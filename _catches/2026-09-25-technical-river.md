Oku called this "the most technical fishing in Hokkaido." Deep, slow pools with giant rainbows posted up against the bank under overhanging grass. A few inches off and you're snagged in the grass or you've missed the spot, and in water that clear and still a bad presentation spooks them instantly.

> Mending: the casts cross currents moving at different speeds, and if any part of the line drags the fly, the gig is up.

Caught a really nice rainbow and a few smaller ones. They all fight hard.

It was interesting watching the big rainbows near the bank rising, I tried quite hard to cast to them, but in retrospect (after fishing for more weeks), I think the placement needs to be like right next to the grass for them to want to go for it. And missing a single cast really messes with probability of catching one afterwards. 

I eventually fished a more choppy area, this involved blind casting and I was able to catch some nice rainbows here. But other than there it was difficult.
