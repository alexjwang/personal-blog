The best day of fishing of all time. It was certainly the best day of fishing that my guide has seen! This day is going to be hard to beat in my lifetime, even if I go fishing all the time. Just the raw number of giant fish I caught, the overall experience, and breaking my personal best rainbow all in one sitting.

We started later on account of the overnight rain (8:30) just to gauge weather conditions and also fish in a little more comfortable conditions. When we finally got to the spot, we saw a ton of cars but honestly not many fly fishermen. While we were getting set up, I saw a ton of bugs running around (kamemushi, grasshoppers, etc) which was a really good sign for fish activity today. And of course right before we got to the river, my guide realized we forgot to get our fishing passes for the day. Oops!

So round 2, we're back at the river. There's actually a tour group or something there, where there's one Japanese guide and a bunch of Americans(?) fly fishing that stretch of river. One guy maybe has some experience, the others were clearly total beginners, but all of them definitely were just starting out. Again, I was sorta expecting that the fish would be spooked by the large number of them slapping fly line on the surface of the water, but we managed to access a deeper pool that none of them were fishing.

For nymphing, I took off the indicator again (so only sightline) which I feel like is the fly fishing equivalent of taking off training wheels. After running my nymph through the pool a few times, I figured out how the current and depths worked, and I decided how I wanted to cast/let the nymph drift. Essentially the current ran super fast into this deep pocket before crashing into a 90 degree turn in the river (including some fallen logs), so there was a bit of current bouncing back from the far bank, creating a second current that looped back into the main one. Where that ricochet current hit the main current was where slack water existed, and it also happened to be the deepest part of the pool, right next to a very steep dropoff. I let my nymph drift downstream in the main current, bounce with the ricocheting current, and settle into that deep slack pool. I watched it slowly drift, and then the line ever so slightly straightened out and stopped. That was all the signal I needed--fish on! The moment I hooked into the fish, even before it started struggling, I knew this was something much larger than anything I'd caught before. Just the sheer lack of movement when I set the hook told me that this thing had mass. When it started running with the current, all the bad memories of lost fish in the past came flooding into my mind, and I maintained tension in the line like my life depended on it, frantically reeling up line to match the speed at which the fish was running. The fish turned in the water, revealing a big white flash deep in the pool, at which point my guide also realized how big this fish was. Landing that fish was one of the happiest moments of my life, not just because I had been searching for that 60cm milestone for such a long time, but also because I knew that I could not have caught that fish even a few days ago. That fish was insane, I think most fly fishermen probably go their entire lives without catching a trout that large.

The day just kept going, I hooked into 4 more giant rainbows in that same spot, each of which would've been my personal best rainbow on any other day (50cm+). And to think the group was right there fishing the same spot too! It felt good to know I had progressed past beginner status and was now capable of catching these trout.

We went further down river, where I caught a large iwana. I was told that 30cm iwana (shaku) were also considered a trophy fish (it's hard for them to grow that large), and in that first spot I managed to catch one! The fish didn't stop there, I caught another 2 rainbows, both of which fought quite hard for their size. I was utterly convinced that last one was going to be 60cm as well, but I think he was just particularly feisty.

We took a lunch break at a ramen stop before going to the lake for some new experiences. There, I mostly practiced throwing streamers while enjoying the lake scenery. There was this sandy slope around the mouth of the river, but at the end, the sand drops off into oblivion. I took maybe one step too much and almost slipped off into the abyss (the sand didn't have very good traction to help backpedal). That could've been like really bad.

While fishing, we could see big fish rising around us. Then, all of a sudden, I saw a fish dolphin through the air as it jumped out of the water for something. For a split second I could see the entire body of the fish in the air, and it was utterly massive. That silhouette is burned forever into my mind as one of the coolest things I've ever seen. And the jumps didn't end there, I watched that fish soar through the air, chasing it futilely with my streamer/dry fly. I swapped to a streamer that Oku had given to me a week ago (it's called the Alexandra fly, he gave it to me because my name sorta matched and I had caught several nice trout with it). After a very short while, I cast at the big fish rising, and hooked into a fish! I could tell it wasn't quite a monster, but it was definitely a nice sized fish. And as it came in, we could see it wasn't a rainbow. In fact, it was the last species on my list that I hadn't caught yet--amemasu! Or maybe technically iwana since it never went to the ocean? In any case, this fish felt almost as cool as the giant rainbow from earlier. Just another bucket list fish that I had been chasing after this entire trip, and the memory of standing out in the lake with all the giant fish jumping around me with the backdrop of the mountains in the distance is a tough one to beat in any number of lifetimes!
